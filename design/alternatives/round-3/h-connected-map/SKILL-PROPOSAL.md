# Proposed portable explanation instructions

Status: review draft. No installed skill is changed, and no presentation has been selected.

## Start with the understanding the reader needs

Identify the reader's task, prior knowledge and consequential questions. Read the available implementation, requirements and recorded observations before choosing a visual structure. Preserve settled decisions and stable requirement identifiers. Record missing essential evidence without inventing behavior to complete a picture.

Choose a representation that exposes the relationships the reader needs: sequence for order, branches for concurrent or conditional work, ownership for handoffs, comparison for alternatives, and state or lifetime for what persists. Combine views only when each answers a distinct question. No diagram type, number of groups or page skeleton is mandatory.

## Make the overview a useful map

If the reader needs a whole-system account, include its relevant entry conditions, work, outputs and continuation. Use a compact overview with consistent labels linking to necessary closeups. Explain consequential boundaries where they occur: what crosses, who controls the next action and what the successful handoff actually establishes.

Name actions with familiar project or domain terms. Explain unfamiliar terms at first use. Keep implementation symbols in secondary evidence unless the reader needs them to act. Label visual groupings as groupings when their boundaries do not correspond to real entities. Explain arrow direction, containment and ordering where they could be misunderstood.

Show independent work side by side and its join explicitly when concurrency matters. Attach an exception to the step that detects it and state the resulting consequence. Preserve those relationships when the layout reflows. Avoid using one grouping to imply that ownership, lifetime, change frequency and implementation status share the same boundary.

## Connect evidence to claims

Use actual interface captures when the visible product helps explain an action. Preserve provenance and distinguish synthetic data from a synthetic interface. Place annotations on the relevant controls without obscuring them, and connect the annotations to the same action names used in the explanation. A captured screen establishes visible state; it does not establish unseen processing or later success.

Label illustrations, inferred explanations, proposals and observed behavior where each appears. Include source references near material claims and optional excerpts when useful for offline review. Separate the implemented mechanism, its intended benefit and evidence that the benefit occurs. Make an unknown outcome visible at the affected boundary.

## Keep the first reading complete

Expose essential facts without interaction. Use controls for optional evidence or meaningful exploration after the reader can already follow the account. Keep closeups focused on the questions the overview cannot answer at its scale, rather than repeating the entire narrative in several forms.

For a material unresolved decision, state the current behavior, concrete situation, alternatives, tradeoffs and recommendation. Preserve stable option identifiers and an explicit answer state. A recommendation is not an approval. Do not invent decisions to populate a template or reopen settled choices.

## Verify comprehension conditions

Inspect the artifact at the intended screen sizes and with required scripts unavailable when appropriate. Check reading order, labels, connectors, capture legibility, overflow, links, contrast and optional controls. Verify that the reader can trace the relevant causal chain and distinguish present behavior from proposed outcomes. Rendering evidence establishes usability checks; reader feedback determines whether the explanation communicates successfully.

Revise the representation in response to the actual misunderstanding. Keep examples grounded in the current domain while expressing reusable guidance through reader needs, relationships and evidence.
